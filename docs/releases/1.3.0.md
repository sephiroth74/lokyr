Programs that tell you what they're doing, a click that moves the shell's cursor, and tabs that keep up: pinned, grouped, reopened and stepped through in the order you used them; hints to open what's on the screen without the mouse.

## What's new since 1.2.0

- **Programs' status on their tabs**: a program that tells its state with OSC 7501 shows it on its tab exactly: at work, waiting for you, of what it asks for, done, or failed. A program's status of its session, OSC 21337, shows as a dot of its color, its text in the tab's tooltip. Desktop notifications of OSC 99 show as those of OSC 9 and 777 do.
- **A click in the command line** moves the shell's cursor there, as in a text field, in zsh, bash and fish with Lokyr's shell integration, and in any shell that announces it. Backspace, Delete or a typed character over a selection of the command line takes its place. `[cursor] click-to-move` turns both off.
- **⌃Tab shows the tab you used before**: keep Control down and press Tab again to go further back. ⌃⇧Tab goes the other way. `[plugins.tabs] control-tab = "order"` keeps the bar's order.
- **Pinned tabs**: right-click a tab and choose Pin Tab. It stays at the start of the bar, narrow, and ⌘W doesn't close it.
- **Tab groups**: right-click a tab and choose New Group with Tab to gather tabs under a name and a color. Click the group's pill to fold it; drag tabs in and out of it. Restored windows keep their pins and groups.
- **Reopen a closed tab**: Shell › Reopen Closed Tab, ⇧⌘T, brings back the tab you closed last, as you left it, for 10 seconds after you close it. `[plugins.tabs] reopen-seconds` sets the time.
- **Hints**: press ⇧⌘E to label every URL, path, git hash and IP address on the screen, then type a label to open it, or a capital to copy it. Add your own patterns, such as a ticket's number to its page, in the settings window's Links › Hints.
- **The quick terminal from a double tap**: set `[plugins.quick-terminal] double-tap` to a modifier, then press it twice quickly, alone, from any app.
- **Use Transparency**: press ⌘U to show a translucent window opaque while you read, and again to see through it.
- **Text fields** have a context menu of Cut, Copy, Paste and Select All, and take macOS's Control-T, Control-P, Control-N and Control-Y.
- **The settings link each key's topic** in the documentation, beside its More info.
- **Plugins**: the plugin API 0.1.5 reads the tabs in the order they were used, pins, groups and reopens tabs, reads a program's status and what a waiting agent asks for, labels a decoration, and hears a modifier's double tap.

## Changed

- ⌃Tab and ⌃⇧Tab follow the order you used the tabs in. ⇧⌘] and ⇧⌘[ still follow the bar.
- An agent's notification tells its own message, or what it asks for while it waits.
- The scroll indicator of a list shows while you scroll it and fades a moment after, as macOS's scroll bars do.
- The settings window's sidebar is wider, its search field and its sections lined up.

## Fixed

- The text fields take their editing keys before Lokyr's shortcuts: Shift-Command-← selects to the field's start, and Command-↑ moves the caret.
- A command's mark in the gutter shows on its prompt's first line of text, not on an empty line that a prompt theme prints first.
- A tab's context menu waits for a click on an item, rather than running the one under the pointer.
- The blinking cursor no longer shows the character of another cell for a moment after a quick move.
- A plugin whose command fails while you answer its earlier question can ask you again.

## Before you update

- Plugins installed for 1.2.0 keep working: the plugin API is 0.1.5, which takes 0.1.4's plugins.
- The first time you set a modifier's double tap, macOS asks you to allow Lokyr in System Settings › Privacy & Security › Input Monitoring. Lokyr reads the modifiers alone, never what you type.
