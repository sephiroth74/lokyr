## What's new

- **Tabs**: ⌘T opens a tab, ⌘1 to ⌘9 select one, ⇧⌘] and ⇧⌘[ the next and the previous, and ⌘W closes it. The tab bar sits in the titlebar by default, or at the bottom, or in a column on the left or the right, in three looks, `standard`, `unified` and `seamless`, as `[plugins.tabs]` in the config says. Each tab shows its title, the keys that select it, its color and a close button, and tells you what happens in it while you look elsewhere: new output, a bell, a program that runs, Secure Keyboard Entry, and a program's progress round its outline.
- **Tabs with the mouse**: a middle click closes a tab, a double click renames it, and its context menu renames it, closes it, or closes the others. Drag a tab to reorder it, to another window's bar, onto a pane, which it splits, or out of the window, into a window of its own; Escape puts it back.
- **A single tab** hides the bar, and the window shows its title and its marks in the titlebar.
- **Split panes**: ⌘D splits the pane to the right, ⇧⌘D down; ⌥⌘ and an arrow move to the pane beside, ⌘] and ⌘[ to the next and the previous; ⌃⌘ and an arrow resize, and ⇧⌘Return zooms a pane to the whole tab. Shell › Equalize Panes shares the room again, and a pane moves out to a tab or a window of its own. Drag a divider to resize, or double-click it to share half and half. The panes without the keyboard fade a little; `[plugins.splits]` sets how much, shows each pane's title, and has the keyboard follow the pointer.
- **Find**: ⌘F opens a find bar at the top right of the pane, of your last search, selected, so that typing replaces it. It searches the screen and the scrollback as you type, highlights every match that shows, and counts them; Return and ⌘G go to the next match, upwards, ⇧Return and ⇧⌘G to the previous one, and the view follows, even while a program prints on. Its toggles match case, whole words and regular expressions, and its magnifying glass lists your recent searches. Escape closes it, the current match selected, ready for ⌘C.
- **Windows by number**: ⌥⌘1 to ⌥⌘9 bring a window forward, its number shown in its titlebar, which `title-shortcut = false` under `[window]` hides.
- **Quitting with the last window**: `quit-after-last-window-closed = true` under `[app]` quits Lokyr as its last window closes. Lokyr stays open by default, as macOS's apps do.
- **Your own keys from nothing**: the default shortcuts are a plugin of their own, which `enabled = false` under `[plugins.keymap-macos]` turns off, so that your `[keys]` bind every key.

## Works better

- A program that follows the mouse, as an editor's drag of a selection, keeps the drag in its pane until you release the button, wherever the pointer goes.
- Moving between panes tells their programs that they gained or lost the keyboard, as editors use to refresh, and each tab shows the title of its pane that has the keyboard.
- The underline of a link under a still pointer, while you hold ⌘, goes as soon as its text changes or scrolls away.

## Before you update

- ⌘W now closes the tab, or its pane in a tab of several; ⇧⌘W closes the whole window.
